Lab 19–22b — Quantization Error, Measured

Lab thesis: a b-bit quantizer adds an error you can predict from its step size alone, and every bit removed costs about 6 dB of signal-to-noise ratio. A model's accuracy does not track that number: a small classifier keeps its accuracy far below the bit width where a signal looks badly distorted, then collapses. This lab measures both.

It follows Lab 19–22a, where you build the quantizers, and is the experimental half of Lecture 19/20 — Number formats and Lecture 21/22 — Affine quantization. You quantize with the affine map from Lecture 21/22 §1, and the notebook gives the two error measures you need.

Runs on Google Colab (free) or your own machine, in under a minute on any CPU. It needs only numpy, matplotlib and scikit-learn; there is nothing to download.

Open the notebook in Colab · read it on this site · download from GitHub

The two rules you will measure

Rule Where it comes from
MSE ≈ S² / 12 rounding moves each value by at most S/2, spread evenly across one step
about 6 dB of SNR per bit one more bit halves S, which quarters the error power: 10 · log10(4) ≈ 6

What you do

Set ROLL_NUMBER and NAME first. Your roll number seeds your signals, your data split and your model, so your numbers are yours, and that is what the plagiarism check reads. You implement the four functions marked # TODO; the plots and harnesses are given.

Function Does
quantize(x, bits) the affine map: stretch, scale, zero-point, round and clip. Returns (q, S, Z)
dequantize(q, S, Z) S (q − Z)
snr_db(x, x_hat) signal power over error power, in dB
quantize_per_channel(w, bits) one (S, Z) per output column of a weight matrix

Part 1 — The error playground (55 marks)

  • 1a. Look at one quantizer. A sine at 3 bits, drawn over the histogram of its values with the 8 levels marked.
  • 1b. Sweep the bits. A sine and Gaussian noise at 1–8 bits: MSE against S² / 12, and SNR against bits.
  • 1c. One outlier. One sample of the sine set to 20, quantized at 8 bits with one (S, Z) for the whole signal, then in blocks of 64 (Lecture 21/22 §5).

The paragraphs: where S² / 12 breaks down and why; the dB-per-bit rule in one sentence; how many bits one outlier wasted, and what blocks of 64 cost in storage.

Part 2 — Quantize a small model (45 marks)

A one-hidden-layer network classifies 8×8 handwritten digits (scikit-learn's bundled digits set). It trains in about a second; after that it is two weight matrices and two bias vectors. You quantize only the weights, per-tensor and per-channel, at 1–8 bits, and the harness measures test accuracy. Nothing is retrained.

  • 2a. Per-tensor vs per-channel. Where accuracy falls apart, and why per-channel holds on longer.
  • 2b. Connect the two parts. At 3 bits the weights' SNR is low enough to call a sine badly distorted, yet the classifier barely notices. Why?

What you submit

Two files. The JSON carries your measurements; the notebook carries your reasoning.

  1. submission_lab19_22b_<roll>.json, written by the notebook's final cell. Set ROLL_NUMBER and NAME first.
  2. The executed notebook, with outputs and plots intact and every 📝 prediction and explanation cell filled in.

How it is marked

Automatic (from your JSON). Each check tests a relationship between your own numbers, never a magnitude against a reference:

Relationship Why it holds
MSE / (S² / 12) between 0.8 and 1.25 at 4–8 bits rounding error spread evenly over one step
SNR slope 5.5–7 dB per bit, sine and noise one bit halves S
sine SNR > noise SNR at every width the sine fills its range; the noise's rare extremes set S
outlier drop within 3 dB of 20 · log10(range ratio) S grows with the range
blocks of 64 recover at least 10 dB a smaller group confines the outlier
your forward pass matches scikit-learn's accuracy the model really is two matrices and two biases
8-bit accuracy within 0.01 of float 8-bit error is far below what the model notices
1-bit near chance; 2-bit per-tensor below float too few levels to keep the weights apart
per-channel: at most 4 levels in any column at 2 bits, accuracy ≥ per-tensor each output column has its own S
per-channel weight SNR > per-tensor at 2–8 bits the widest column no longer sets everyone's S

Rubric (from your written cells). Explanations are marked for naming the mechanism: the step size, the range that sets it, the outlier, the argmax. The heaviest items are 1b's dB-per-bit rule, 1c's outlier in bits, and 2b's paragraph connecting the two parts.

40 automatic + 60 rubric, out of 100. The automatic half is marked by code/quantization/grade_lab19_22b.py from the JSON your notebook writes.

An honest surprising measurement, correctly explained, scores higher than a tidy expected one. If your model kept its accuracy at 2 bits, or per-channel lost to per-tensor at some width, say so and explain why.

Academic integrity

The signals, the split and the model are seeded from your roll number, so two honest submissions never share a measurement vector. Submissions sharing that vector are treated as copied. Discussing mechanisms is encouraged; sharing measurement files is not.
